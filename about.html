About Me

I am a data professional currently pursuing my second master’s degree. My work is grounded in statistical learning and predictive modeling, but my guiding perspective is that effective data science begins with problem framing.

Before selecting tools or models, I focus on understanding the underlying question, and the purpose of the analysis. I prioritize asking clarifying questions, defining parameters and identifying what a meaningful answer should accomplish. I approach tools and methods as interchangeable means rather than fixed identities. I am comfortable adapting to new environments and selecting techniques based on fit, whether working in Python or R, or choosing between approaches such as logistic or ordinal regression. My emphasis is on aligning analytical decisions with problem structure and translating results into insights that support informed decision-making.

AI is a core part of how I work, not a shortcut around it. I've built multi-agent systems directly on the Anthropic SDK — not a wrapper framework — for both my 990 financial analysis agent and a validation system I designed for a Wisconsin Women's Council dashboard project. I use Claude as a technical collaborator across the full data-engineering and BI lifecycle: schema design, SQL and DAX debugging, documentation. At Delta Defense, I use enterprise AI copilot tooling for SQL generation and warehouse queries. But the part I care about most is that I don't accept AI output on faith — I verify every AI-generated query, fix, or figure against primary source data before it ships, the same way I'd check a junior collaborator's work rather than treat it as an oracle. That habit has caught real bugs, including a fabricated source-data code from one model and a wrong-variable query from an internal tool. More than anything, AI has given me a kind of superpower: the ability to actually keep pace with the curiosity that drives me, chasing down questions and building things I wouldn't have had the bandwidth to pursue otherwise.

In addition to my academic and research work, I am also a student athlete on the UW–Milwaukee women’s track and field team. This experience has shaped my work ethic, discipline, and ability to manage time effectively while balancing demanding commitments.

I am always looking for new challenges and ways to expand my skills. A quote from Socrates that has always stayed with me is
“I only know one thing, and that is I know nothing.”
because it pushes me to keep asking questions and continue growing.
